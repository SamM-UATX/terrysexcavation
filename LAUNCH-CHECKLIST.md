# Post-Launch Checklist

Practical next steps once the site is live. None of this is required for the
website code itself to work — it's the marketing/SEO follow-through that makes
the site actually generate calls.

## 1. Domain

- Domain used throughout the site: `terrysexcavation.com` — **registration/ownership
  not confirmed**, unlike the old `terrysbobcatpoolremoval.com`. Register/confirm it
  first, then connect it to Netlify per the README's GitHub → Netlify deploy steps.
- Once live, double check every canonical/OG URL and the sitemap actually resolve on
  the real domain (see README).

## 2. Google Search Console

- Verify the domain in [Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console).
- Submit `https://<yourdomain>/sitemap.xml`.
- Check the "Pages" report after a week or two to confirm all 11 pages get indexed.

## 3. Analytics

- Add a privacy-friendly analytics tool (e.g. [Plausible](https://plausible.io) or
  [Fathom](https://usefathom.com)) or Google Analytics if preferred. Not installed
  yet — this site currently ships with zero third-party trackers, which also helps
  page speed and privacy.

## 4. Google Business Profile

- Claim/create a listing at [business.google.com](https://business.google.com)
  under **Terry's Excavation**, matching the site's name, phone, and (once decided)
  address exactly (NAP consistency). Resolve the business-name/DBA question (see
  README) before doing this — Google Business Profile checks license/name matches.
- This is typically the single biggest lever for local map-pack rankings — higher
  priority than most of the other items on this list.

## 5. Sitemap submission

- Covered by step 2 (Search Console). Also worth submitting to Bing Webmaster Tools.

## 6. Local citations

- Add/update the same business name, phone, and address on Yelp, Angi, BBB, and
  Nextdoor. Consistency across all of these (and the Google Business Profile) is
  what search engines use to trust a local business's legitimacy.

## 7. Review collection

- Once jobs wrap up, ask customers for a Google review — a short "text this link
  after the job" message works well. Replace the homepage's "more reviews coming
  soon" placeholder with real reviews as they come in, and only add review
  structured data (schema.org `Review`/`AggregateRating`) once there are enough
  genuine reviews to be eligible under Google's policies.

## 8. Google Local Services Ads / Search Ads

- Once the Google Business Profile is verified, evaluate
  [Local Services Ads](https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads/) (pay-per-lead,
  well-suited to a licensed home-service contractor) and/or standard Search Ads for
  high-intent terms like "pool removal San Jose."

## 9. Before-and-after social reels

- Instagram and Facebook: short before/after clips of pool removals perform well
  for this trade. The gallery page's finished-transformation photos are a good
  starting library; new jobs should get short video clips going forward.
- Priority order suggested: Google Business Profile first, then Instagram +
  Facebook, then Nextdoor (strong hyper-local trust signal).

## 10. Ongoing gallery & content updates

- Add new job photos regularly (see README's "Adding more real photos" section) —
  fresh, real photo content is a meaningful local-SEO signal for Google and keeps
  the gallery genuinely representative of current work.
- Revisit the service-area list if Terry starts actively serving new cities —
  add them deliberately, not preemptively.
