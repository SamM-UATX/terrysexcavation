Swimming Pool Removal

Every pool removal comes down to three stages: opening up the shell, taking out however much concrete your project calls for, and putting the ground back together so the yard is actually usable afterward. Which combination fits your property depends on your city's requirements and what you're planning for the space — Terry walks through the options with you during your free estimate.

Stage 1 — Partial or cave-in demolition

The most common starting point. The upper bond beam and coping — typically the top 18 to 36 inches of the shell — are broken up, and large holes are punched through the remaining concrete so groundwater can't collect underneath after backfill. From there, you have two paths:

  • Cave-in: the most budget-friendly option. Only the top portion comes out; the rest of the shell is broken up and left in place, buried under compacted backfill.
  • Partial removal: more concrete is broken up and reduced than a straight cave-in, balancing cost against stricter city requirements.

Good fit if…

You want the most cost-effective option and you're not planning heavy construction directly over the old pool footprint. Whether cave-in or partial removal applies to your property depends on your city's permit requirements, confirmed during the estimate and permitting process.

Stage 2 — Complete pool removal

The full job: the entire concrete shell — walls, floor, and bond beam — is broken into haulable pieces and extracted, with every load of concrete and rebar leaving your property in our own trucks. Nothing old stays buried, which matters if you're planning to build over the footprint later.

Good fit if…

You're planning a structure (like an ADU) directly over the old pool footprint, or your city code requires full extraction rather than cave-in or partial removal.

Stage 3 — Backfill and compaction

This is the stage that determines whether your yard stays flat and usable or starts sinking a year later. Soil is added back into the cavity in shallow layers — not dumped in all at once — and each layer is compacted with a jumping-jack tamper before the next one goes in. Working layer by layer like this is what keeps the ground stable enough for grass, a garden, a patio, or a future structure.

Why it matters

Skipping proper compaction is the single biggest cause of a sunken yard after pool removal. Final grading and a city inspection sign-off follow once backfill is complete.

Every property is different. Permit requirements, allowed removal methods, and drainage needs vary by city and by property. We confirm the specifics for your job during your free estimate and the permitting process — nothing here should be taken as a guarantee of what your city or property will require.
